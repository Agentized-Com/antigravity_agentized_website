Best AI Consultants for Small Businesses (2026): Costs, Red Flags, and How to Choose

Updated October 30, 2026 · By Mark Fidelman, Co-founder, Agentized, and co-author of the upcoming book AGENT SHOCK · Published by Agentized, which is one of the options below. See "How we selected these options."

Most small businesses don't need an AI consultant on day one. The simple, practical first step is to take one repetitive task, like answering the same customer emails, scheduling or data entry, and try a free or low-cost tool on it. A consultant earns the fee when the task touches several of your systems, when nobody on your team has time to set it up, or when a mistake would cost real money. This guide covers free help to try first, the red flags to watch for, and 7 options compared.

TL;DR

  • Try free first: SCORE, an SBA resource partner, gives small business owners free mentoring, and your local Small Business Development Center can point you to AI training.
  • For one simple automation on a small budget: a vetted freelance AI specialist.
  • For a Chicago-based technology partner that helps growing businesses implement technology "for less": Xcelacore.
  • For applied AI on Salesforce, Azure, Snowflake or AWS: RTS Labs.
  • For machine learning, data engineering or document AI: Addepto.
  • For a custom app or AI agent built from scratch: LeewayHertz.
  • For an established business around $25 million in revenue or more, with a measurable revenue or cost problem: Agentized.

How we selected these options

We are Agentized, an AI consulting firm, so we have a stake in this list. To keep it useful:

  • Every firm here was named for small businesses when we asked Claude, ChatGPT and Gemini 50 common AI questions as a small business owner (October 2026).
  • Every description was checked against the firm's own website. Where an AI answer said something the firm's site did not support, such as a price, we left it out.
  • The free options come first, then firms alphabetically, not ranked.
  • Every entry has a con, including ours. Ours is that most small businesses are too small for us.

We judged each option on five things a small business owner should check:

  1. Cost you can see up front. Is there a clear setup price and monthly cost?
  2. Simple, practical results. Will it save time on a real, repetitive task within weeks?
  3. Uses the tools you already pay for. Does it work with your email, CRM, accounting or booking software?
  4. Something you can run yourself. Will you be able to run it after they leave?
  5. Honest about when you don't need them. Do they tell you when a free tool would do?

The options

SCORE and SBA resource partners (free)

SCORE is an SBA resource partner whose mentors give small business owners advice "at no cost" on topics like financing, hiring and business planning. The SBA's local assistance tool also finds your nearest Small Business Development Center, which is where most federal small business AI training is being routed.

Pros: free; local; no sales pitch.

Cons: general business advice, not someone who will build the automation for you.

Pricing: free.

Best for: deciding whether you need paid help at all.

Freelance AI specialist

A vetted freelancer can set up one simple automation, like routing leads from your website form into your CRM with an AI-drafted reply, for far less than a firm.

Pros: cheapest paid option; fast for a single task.

Cons: quality varies; nobody to call when it breaks unless you agree on support up front.

Pricing: varies widely; insist on a fixed quote.

Best for: one repetitive task on a small budget.

Addepto

Addepto is an AI and big data company offering AI consulting, generative AI development, data engineering, computer vision and MLOps, plus its ContextClue knowledge-management product. It works under solution-providing, collaborative or managed delivery models.

Pros: deep technical range.

Cons: built around data and machine learning projects, which is more than a simple automation needs.

Pricing: not published.

Addepto is best for: small businesses with a real data or machine learning problem.

LeewayHertz, part of The Hackett Group, offers AI development, machine learning, data engineering, custom software and AI agent development, and serves clients from startups to Fortune 500 companies.

Pros: deep engineering; works with startups.

Cons: an AI engineering firm first, not a practical small business automation shop.

LeewayHertz is best for: a custom app or AI agent that off-the-shelf tools can't do.

RTS Labs is a "boutique applied AI firm" for high-growth companies, offering agentic AI, AI integration, data engineering and software engineering on Salesforce, Azure, Snowflake and AWS.

Pros: applied and build-focused; works on common business platforms.

Cons: aimed at high-growth companies in financial services, insurance, logistics, real estate and construction, so a very small business may be below its usual size.

Pricing: not published.

RTS Labs is best for: growing businesses adding AI to the platforms they already use.

Xcelacore

Xcelacore is a Chicago-based firm that helps businesses implement technology, including AI and machine learning, and says it does so "for less" than building in-house. Its own guide to small business AI consultants lists time-and-materials, fixed-price and outcome-based pricing models.

Pros: cost-conscious positioning; flexible engagement models.

Cons: its site names few specific AI offerings, so ask for an AI example close to your task.

Pricing: not published.

Xcelacore is best for: growing businesses that want one ongoing technology partner.

How to choose an AI consultant for a small business

  1. Pick one repetitive task first. Choose one that eats hours every week: customer emails, quotes, scheduling, invoices or data entry.
  2. Try a free or cheap tool on it yourself. If it works, you may not need a consultant at all.
  3. Ask for a fixed price and a monthly cost. Get both the setup fee and what you'll pay every month for tools and support.
  4. Ask to see a similar small business they helped. Ask for the same size and the same kind of task.
  5. Make sure you own it. Everything should be built in your own accounts (your CRM, your AI subscription), not theirs.
  6. Agree on what "done" means. Write down the task, the result you expect and how you'll check it before any work starts.

Red flags

  • They won't give a price until after a long paid "discovery."
  • They build everything in their own accounts, so you can't leave.
  • They can't name a small business they've helped, or what it saved.
  • They push a custom build when a free or low-cost tool would do.
  • They promise results without asking how the task works today.
  • They have no plan for who fixes it when it breaks.

How do I choose an AI consultant for a small business?

Pick one repetitive task, try a free tool first, then choose the consultant who gives a fixed price and a monthly cost, has helped a similar small business, and builds in your own accounts.

How much does AI consulting cost for a small business?

None of the firms on this list publish prices, and freelance rates vary widely. Get a fixed quote for one defined task, plus the monthly cost of the tools it needs, from at least two options.

What are the red flags when hiring an AI consultant?

No price up front, building in their own accounts, no similar small business references, pushing a custom build when a free tool would do, and no plan for support.

Do I need an AI consultant, or can I do it myself?

For one simple, repetitive task, often you can do it yourself with a free or low-cost tool. A consultant is worth it when the task touches several systems, nobody on your team has time, or mistakes are costly.

Are there free AI resources for small businesses?

Yes. SCORE mentors give free advice as an SBA resource partner, and the SBA's local assistance tool finds your nearest Small Business Development Center.

Final word

The best AI consultant for a small business is the one that solves one repetitive, expensive task at a price you can see up front, and leaves you owning the result. Try free first, then shortlist two or three options from the table above and compare their fixed prices, monthly costs and references side by side.
